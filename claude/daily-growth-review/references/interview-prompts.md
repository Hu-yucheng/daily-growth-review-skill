# Progressive Interview Prompts

Ask only the highest-value missing question. Use facts already supplied and produce a useful draft after no more than five review questions.

## First-use profile

Ask one question at a time:

1. What roles or life stage most shape your days right now?
2. What one to three changes matter most in your current 2–12 week cycle?
3. What constraints and resources most affect your ability to act?
4. What feedback style helps you, and what topics should the review avoid?

Sensitive context is optional. Explain why it matters before asking. Prefer “How does caregiving affect your available time?” over “Tell me about your whole family.”

## Daily triage

- What were the day's main blocks, even approximately?
- What concrete output or evidence exists?
- Where did energy rise, fall, or recover?
- What created friction, avoidance, or conflict?
- If tomorrow had one primary outcome, what consequence makes it the priority?

## Gentle mode

- What did today require from you that is easy to overlook?
- What is one small piece of real progress?
- What minimum action would preserve continuity without causing further harm?

## Deep mode

- What happened immediately before the behavior?
- What assumption or feared consequence was active?
- Did the failure come from capability, opportunity, motivation, environment, or task design?
- What similar situation can provide a fair comparison?

## Strict mode

Use only when the profile requests it. Be direct about behavior and cost, never identity.

- Which observable action was avoidance rather than progress?
- Which “reasonable” activity displaced the primary outcome?
- What must be removed, reduced, or externally checked tomorrow?

Never diagnose, humiliate, threaten, or treat pain as deserved.

## Stop asking

Stop interviewing when the answer would not change the event heart, score, comparison, or next action. Mark missing facts as uncertainty instead of continuing an intake indefinitely.
